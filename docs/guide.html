The guide

Turn the device you already own into a TV box.

You don't need new hardware. An old phone, a tablet, a Windows PC or the TV stick you already have can be a clean, ad-free TV. This page explains the whole idea in plain English: how to install apps from a file, how to get the picture on a big screen, how to control it from the couch, and what kinds of apps to put on it.

The idea

A TV box is four things.

Every streaming box, however it is sold, is the same four parts. You probably own all of them already.

01 · BrainA device that runs Android

A phone or tablet on Android 7.1 or newer, an Android TV or Google TV box, a Fire TV on Fire OS 6 or newer, or a Windows PC running an Android emulator.

02 · ScreenA way to reach the TV

A cable (USB-C to HDMI) or wireless casting. A tablet on a stand works as a bedside or kitchen TV with no TV at all.

03 · ControlSomething to hold

A Bluetooth remote, game controller or keyboard. Touch still works, but nobody wants to walk to the TV to press pause.

04 · HomeA screen built for the couch

This is the part phones are missing, and it is what Phone TV adds: big tiles, your apps in a row, and remote-friendly navigation instead of a grid of tiny icons.

Quick start

The short version.

Install the home screen

On the device, download PhoneTV.apk and open it. Android will ask you to allow the install. That is sideloading, explained below.

Make it Home

Press Home and choose Phone TV, or open it and use Settings › Make Phone TV my Home screen.

Connect the screen

Plug in an HDMI adapter or start casting. Turn the phone sideways.

Pair a remote

Put the remote in pairing mode and add it under the device's Bluetooth settings.

Add your apps

Install what you watch with, then long-press a tile to favorite it so it sits on the top shelf.

Phone TV running in landscape.

Honest limits. Phone TV is a home screen, not a video service. It does not host or stream anything; it opens the apps you install. Some services limit video quality on phones, uncertified devices or emulators, and that is decided by the service, not the launcher.

Sideloading

Installing an app from a file.

Android apps are packaged as .apk files. An app store downloads and installs that file for you. Sideloading just means doing it yourself: get the APK, open it, confirm. It is a normal Android feature, and it is how you install apps a store doesn't carry for your device, including TV editions of apps on a phone.

The one thing Android asks is permission. The first time an app (your browser, a file manager) tries to open an APK, Android shows an Install unknown apps switch for that app. Turn it on, go back, and the install continues.

Phone or tabletStraight from the browser

  1. Open the download link in the device's browser.
  2. Tap the finished download, or find it in the Files app under Downloads.
  3. Allow Install unknown apps when asked, then tap Install.

Samsung: if the install is blocked, pause Auto Blocker under Settings › Security and privacy, then turn it back on afterwards.

Android TV · Google TV · ShieldA downloader app, or a USB stick

  1. From the TV's app store, install a downloader-style app: one that takes a web address and fetches the file.
  2. Under Settings › Apps › Security & restrictions › Unknown sources, allow that app.
  3. Type the APK address, download, install.

No typing: copy the APK to a USB stick, plug it into the box and open it with a file manager app.

Fire TVUnlock Developer options first

  1. Settings › My Fire TV › About. Highlight the device name and press Select seven times.
  2. Back out to My Fire TV › Developer options › Install unknown apps and allow your downloader app.
  3. Type the APK address, download, install.

Newer Fire TV models that run Amazon's Vega OS instead of Fire OS cannot install Android apps at all. Check the model before you buy one for this.

Windows PCAn Android emulator

  1. Install an Android emulator such as BlueStacks.
  2. Drag the APK onto the emulator window, or use its Install APK button.
  3. Open the app and go fullscreen.

Plug the PC into the TV over HDMI and it becomes a lean-back media PC.

From a computer, with ADB

ADB is Google's free command-line tool for talking to Android devices. Turn on Developer options on the device (tap Build number seven times under About), enable USB or network debugging, then:

adb
# TV boxes: connect over Wi-Fi using the IP shown in network settings
$ adb connect 192.168.1.50:5555
# phones: just plug in the USB cable instead
$ adb install -r PhoneTV.apk

Staying safe

  • SRC
    Get APKs from the makerThe developer's own site or source repository. Skip "modded" and "unlocked" copies.
  • SHA
    Check the checksum when one is publishedPhone TV lists its SHA-256 in version.json, and its built-in updater verifies it before installing.
  • OFF
    Switch the permission back offOnce you are done installing, turn Install unknown apps off again for your browser.

Big screen

Getting the picture onto the TV.

BestUSB-C to HDMI

One cable, no lag. The catch: the phone's USB-C port must support video output (called DisplayPort Alt Mode). Most flagship phones do; many budget phones do not. Search your model plus "DisplayPort Alt Mode" before buying an adapter. Pick an adapter with a charging port so the phone stays powered.

No cableWireless casting

Mirror the phone's screen to a smart TV or cast receiver from the quick settings panel (named Cast, Smart View or Screen share depending on the brand). Easy, but expect a slight delay and softer video than a cable.

Older phonesMHL adapters

Some phones from the micro-USB era support MHL, which carries HDMI over the charging port. It only works on models that list MHL support.

No TVThe device is the screen

A tablet on a stand is a complete small TV for a kitchen, desk or bedside. Nothing else to buy.

Already a TV boxNothing to do

Shield, Google TV and Fire TV devices are plugged in already. You are only replacing the home screen.

Desktop modeA note for Samsung

Samsung phones may open DeX when plugged into a display. Switch the phone to screen mirroring in the notification that appears if you want the TV to show the phone's own screen instead.

Control

Driving it from the couch.

A TV home screen is navigated with four arrows and an OK button. Anything that can send those works.

  • RMT
    Bluetooth remote or air mouseThe closest thing to a real TV remote. Look for one with a D-pad, Back and Home buttons.
  • PAD
    Game controllerThe D-pad or stick moves, the main button selects. Most Bluetooth controllers pair without any setup.
  • KEY
    KeyboardArrow keys move, Enter selects, Esc goes back. A small wireless keyboard also makes searching and signing in far less painful.
  • APP
    A second phoneRemote-control apps can turn the phone in your hand into a remote for the one behind the TV. Useful as a backup.

Apps to add

What goes on the shelf.

We don't recommend specific apps here. Tastes differ and apps come and go. What stays the same is the job each one does, so think in categories and pick the one you like in each.

Your streaming services

The apps for the subscriptions you already pay for. When a service offers an Android TV edition, prefer it: it is built for a remote and a wide screen, and Phone TV shows its TV banner on the shelf.

A media-server client

If you keep your own movie and music library on a home server, its client app turns that library into your personal streaming service. Phone TV can show Plex rows right on the home screen.

A video player

For files on a USB stick, SD card or network share. A good player handles many formats, subtitles and audio tracks, and remembers where you stopped.

A live TV player

Player apps for the channel lineup from a TV provider you subscribe to, or for free, legal over-the-air and ad-supported channels. The player is only a player; the channels come from your provider.

A file manager

Opens APKs from a USB stick, browses network folders and moves files around. The single most useful utility on a TV device.

A downloader

Takes a web address and saves the file. This is how most people get an APK onto a TV box that has no real browser.

A file-sender

Apps that send files from your everyday phone to the TV device over Wi-Fi, so you can download an APK on the phone and push it across.

A TV-friendly browser

For the occasional website. Choose one made for remotes; ordinary mobile browsers are hard to use without touch.

Music and radio

A TV on a sound system is also the best speaker in the house. Add your music or internet radio app.

Where Phone TV fits. It is the front door for all of the above. Long-press a tile to favorite it, move it, hide it or give it a custom image. Use Tune to type what the featured banner should show, in two or three words.

Always-on tips

Leaving a phone behind the TV.

A phone was designed to live in a pocket, not on a charger for months. A few settings make it a better appliance.

  • BAT
    Limit the chargeIf the phone has a battery-protection or 80–85% charge limit, turn it on. A battery held at 100% wears faster. If the back of the phone ever bulges, stop using it.
  • AIR
    Let it breatheTake the case off and keep it out of direct sun and closed cabinets.
  • NET
    Stay close to the routerUse 5 GHz Wi-Fi where you can. Some USB-C hubs add a wired network port, which helps on phones that support it.
  • DND
    Silence the phone partTurn on Do Not Disturb, remove the SIM if it is a spare, and sign out of accounts you would not want on a shared screen.
  • CLR
    Start cleanA factory reset before you begin gives an old phone its storage and speed back.

Fixes

When something doesn't work.

The APK won't install

Check that Install unknown apps is allowed for the app you opened the file with. On Samsung, pause Auto Blocker. If the message is "App not installed", the download may be incomplete: delete it and download again. An app that says it is not compatible needs a newer Android version than the device has.

Nothing shows on the TV when I plug in the cable

The phone most likely has no video output on its USB-C port. Confirm the model supports DisplayPort Alt Mode. If it does, try another adapter or HDMI cable and make sure the TV is on the right input. Otherwise use wireless casting.

A streaming app plays in low quality, or won't play

Services decide which devices get HD and 4K based on the device's certification, and some refuse emulators entirely. A launcher cannot change that. A certified TV box is the reliable choice for those services; a phone or PC is fine for most others and for your own media.

A TV app opens sideways or ignores touch

TV editions expect a landscape screen and a remote. Rotate the phone and pair a Bluetooth controller or keyboard.

Pressing Home on my TV box goes back to the stock home screen

Android TV, Google TV and Fire TV do not offer a Home app chooser. Phone TV has an experimental setting, Replace the TV home screen, that steps in front of the stock launcher. It is optional and can be paused or turned off at any time. More on TV boxes.

The phone gets warm or slows down

Remove the case, close apps you are not using, and lower the stream quality one step. Casting is harder on a phone than a cable because it encodes video while playing it.
